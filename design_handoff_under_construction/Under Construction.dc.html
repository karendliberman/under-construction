Notice of Motion and Motion to Dismiss

I. INTRODUCTION

Plaintiff's complaint asks this Court to infer a promise from the fact of a negotiation. Stripped of characterisation, the pleading alleges that the parties exchanged drafts, that Defendant declined to sign, and that Plaintiff was disappointed. Under the standard this Court applies at the pleading stage, that is not a claim; it is a description of commercial life.

Counts I and III should be dismissed. Count I fails because the complaint pleads no term that was ever definite enough to enforce. Count III fails because a fraud claim premised on the same conduct is not pleaded with the particularity Rule 9(b) requires — the complaint never identifies who made which statement, or when.

II. STATEMENT OF THE ISSUES

Whether the complaint alleges facts sufficient to establish a contract with definite terms, and whether the fraud allegations satisfy Rule 9(b).

III. LEGAL STANDARD

A complaint must contain sufficient factual matter, accepted as true, to state a claim to relief that is plausible on its face. Legal conclusions couched as factual allegations are not entitled to the presumption of truth, and a court need not accept an inference the pleaded facts do not support.

IV. ARGUMENT

A. Count I fails because no definite term is pleaded. The complaint alleges "an agreement in principle" but identifies no price, no quantity, and no duration. An agreement to continue negotiating is not an enforceable contract, and the complaint's own attachments show the parties contemplated a later signed writing.
